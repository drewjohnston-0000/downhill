# DOWNHILL
### Working Concept Brief

## High Concept

A stylish third-person downhill longboarding game built around **speed, flow, beautiful lines and spectacular landscapes**.

The player begins high in the mountains and descends through forests, villages, winding roads and dense urban environments toward destinations far below.

The experience takes inspiration from the visceral commitment of downhill mountain biking: reading terrain, choosing a line and carrying momentum through a sequence of corners.

It is not primarily a trick game and not a racing simulator.

The fantasy is simply:

**See the road. Pick your line. Commit. Flow down the mountain.**

---

## The Feeling

The player should regularly have moments where the landscape opens in front of them and they can see kilometres of terrain below: switchbacks disappearing through forests, villages clinging to hillsides, a distant city and perhaps the ocean beyond it.

The destination should often be visible long before the player reaches it.

Speed should feel exhilarating rather than merely fast.

A good run should produce the same pleasure as watching an expert downhill mountain biker ride a difficult trail: apparently effortless movement created by constant small decisions.

The ideal emotional mixture is:

**beautiful + fast + slightly frightening + joyful.**

---

## Core Gameplay

The fundamental activity is descending.

Players control lean, carving, braking, tuck and weight distribution while gravity and terrain provide most of the propulsion.

The interesting decisions come from reading what lies ahead:

- corner radius and camber
- road surface
- gradients and compressions
- blind crests
- traffic and pedestrians
- gravel, leaves, water and debris
- alternate lines and shortcuts

Good players increasingly stop reacting to corners and begin anticipating entire sequences.

A satisfying section might feel like:

**carve → apex → tuck → crest → compression → slide → recover → accelerate.**

Mastery is demonstrated through **smoothness and commitment**, not through performing constant tricks.

---

## Flow

The central game state is **flow**.

Flow emerges naturally when the player links clean lines without excessive braking, collisions or loss of momentum.

The game should communicate flow primarily through sensation rather than UI:

- the soundtrack develops
- environmental sound changes
- camera movement becomes more confident
- subtle speed effects appear
- animation becomes more relaxed
- the landscape occasionally opens dramatically around the player

Breaking the line does not necessarily end the run.

A crash costs momentum, rhythm and perhaps dignity — then the player gets back on the board.

The punishment is largely:

**you ruined that beautiful run.**

---

## World Design

Courses should feel like believable places rather than constructed racetracks.

A descent might begin on a mountain road and gradually pass through:

**alpine ridge → forest → switchbacks → hillside village → tunnels → coastal highway → dense town → waterfront**

Routes frequently branch.

The obvious road may be safe and fast, while exploration reveals alternatives:

- alleys
- walking paths
- drainage channels
- abandoned roads
- parking structures
- resort grounds
- construction areas
- dirt trails
- stairways and urban shortcuts

Players gradually learn the mountain in the same way cyclists and downhill riders learn a favourite descent.

Knowledge itself becomes progression.

---

## Discovery

The world should reward curiosity without covering itself in markers and collectibles.

Some of the game's most memorable interactions should be **rare, undocumented and almost pointless**.

For example:

The player takes an unusually close line through a meadow and the rider casually reaches down and plucks a daisy.

On another route, a squirrel happens to run beside the board and the player discovers they can reach down and touch it.

Elsewhere there might be an unexpected high-five, a dog that briefly gives chase, fingers brushing through a fountain, or an interaction nobody has thought to try.

There is no:

**“Secret Interaction 7/40 discovered.”**

The moment simply happens.

Players should occasionally wonder:

**“Wait… did the game actually let me do that?”**

These discoveries should be rare enough that players exchange stories about them — including stories other players initially don't believe.

---

## Style

The visual identity is **downhill skateboard chic against epic scenery**.

Stylised rather than photorealistic.

Characters should have strong silhouettes and expressive physical animation, while environments emphasise:

- huge vistas
- strong geographical forms
- beautiful light
- atmospheric distance
- vegetation in motion
- road texture and surface variation

The world should feel aspirational: somewhere you desperately want to ride.

Fashion, boards, helmets, shoes and riding style provide character expression without overwhelming the central experience.

---

## Tricks

Traditional skateboard tricks can exist, but they are deliberately secondary.

A technically perfect sequence of high-speed corners should feel more impressive than performing repeated kickflips.

Style comes from riding:

deep carving, controlled slides, close passes, unusual lines, little hops, wall touches and confident use of terrain.

The player's movement should look increasingly effortless as their skill increases.

---

## Progression

Progression should primarily expand **possibility rather than numbers**.

Players discover:

- new mountains
- new routes
- hidden connections
- different board setups
- riding styles
- cosmetic equipment

Boards may subtly alter stability, carving response, slide behaviour and speed without becoming a conventional RPG equipment ladder.

The player's biggest improvement should come from actually becoming better at riding.

---

## Technology

**Godot 4.8** is the intended initial development target.

The first prototype should avoid attempting an open world.

Instead, build one carefully composed 2–3 minute descent containing:

**mountain start → forest → technical switchbacks → small settlement → major vista reveal**

The prototype needs to prove only three things:

1. Carving feels wonderful.
2. Speed feels exciting and physical.
3. Seeing the world far below makes the player want to go there.

If those three things work, the fundamental game works.

Everything beyond that is expansion, refinement and content.

---

## Design North Star

Whenever a feature is proposed, ask:

**Does this make descending the mountain feel better?**

If not, it probably doesn't belong in the core game.

The game is ultimately about one very simple pleasure:

**There is a beautiful road going downhill.**

**Go.**
